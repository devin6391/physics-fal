# Cinematic conversion and source coverage

Read when establishing or reviewing a Physics shot before generation, and when assessing the actual take afterward. This guide applies existing Playbook stage responsibilities and Vineet's stated priorities; it does not authorize a new story, look, shot split or production rule.

## What each stage supplies

| Existing work | Information to carry forward |
| --- | --- |
| Approved screenplay | What happens, what is heard, character awareness, required information, emotional intent and cause/effect |
| Assets and Bibles | Locked identity/state, set/object design, geography, character baseline and physical/supernatural context |
| Storyboard | Visual storytelling: staging, viewpoints, sightlines, key poses, actions/reactions and reveals. Strong suggestive evidence; incidental sketch design is not canon. |
| Approved radio cut and cue list | Actual delivery, rhythm, pauses, overlaps and measured event times |
| Animatic | Tested picture/sound progression, visual emphasis, readable durations and intended transitions. A panel push suggests a camera move; it does not establish a navigable 3D space. |
| Cinematic stills/frames | Finished visible appearance at an instant: composition/perspective, materials, light, physical state, pose and expression |
| Cinematic video | The lived performance and continuous physical experience: sustained/evolving emotion and composure, readable face/body expression, purposeful action/contact, camera travel, focus and light behaviour through time |

Do not justify video merely because it repeats an event already communicated by the animatic. Name the necessary performance or continuous visual experience it adds. An expressive still is not necessarily neutral: video sustains or develops its state. Conversely, motion is not automatically valuable; a readable phone insert may fulfil its job as a still. Story and sound timing do not need reinvention during cinematic conversion.

## Source-coverage ledger

For each source below record **relevant part/interval | exact page/file identity | Checked / Reused unchanged evidence / Partial / Unavailable / N/A | evidence and limitation**. Check only relevant material, not the whole film on each request. Explain N/A; access failure is Unavailable. An approved output may answer a question directly without reopening all upstream work, but identify the evidence inherited and return upstream for gaps/conflicts.

- Current Playbook and shot/scene instructions, pending findings and approval state.
- Approved screenplay moment, adjoining beats and original story where needed.
- Full relevant Character Bible entry, scene performance arc and shot Expressions when reviewing performance.
- Relevant World Bible/blueprint geography, story objects and physical/supernatural rules.
- Actual locked character/place/prop references and candidate frames, assigned roles and saved image prompts. Pixels establish what exists; prompt prose establishes intent.
- Scene exposure sheet, measured timing, current plans and dependencies.
- Relevant actual storyboard panels and their staging relationship.
- Identified approved radio cut/cues: use measured cues for event timing; inspect actual audio or an identified verified listening review when judging delivery/audible cues. A transcript/waveform is not hearing.
- Relevant rendered animatic interval, not just its plan, when checking tested staging/flow. Note stale renders and any frame-sampling limitation.
- Adjacent production shots/cut states, and the exact current request or actual candidate take.

The screenplay and approved assets are the creative authorities for verification. Context and derived outputs do not silently override them. An approved supernatural event is not slop because ordinary physics forbids it. Vineet's acceptance of a specific deviation can be recorded for that take; it does not silently rewrite every locked asset.

If actual playback is unavailable, use accessible images, cue/plan data, verified prior observations and technical checks for what they can establish. State exactly what remains unverified. Do not claim to have watched/heard because a player was launched, an audio widget emitted or a file decoded. Missing important evidence produces a partial result or Insufficient evidence, not a fabricated pass.

## Decisions before prose

Record briefly on the existing shot page: **already supplied | still needs direction | conflict/uncertainty**, plus what is intentionally flexible. Every necessary decision needs an owner: inherited evidence, configured control or model-facing instruction. More settings are not inherently better.

| Decision | Useful question |
| --- | --- |
| Dramatic job and performance | What should the audience understand/feel? What does the character want/know? Opening state, intensity and composure; trigger/change or sustained state; closing state. Which face/body/gaze/breathing/voice cues are readable at this shot scale? |
| Framing and optics | Where is the camera relative to the subject? What must remain visible? Inherited shot scale, height/angle/perspective; lens character only where useful. Does depth of field preserve a required background event? What is in focus, and does focus change? Exact focal lengths, apertures and camera brands are optional, not proof of optical correctness. |
| Camera versus subject motion | Starting/ending region, physical route/clearance, speed/easing and stabilisation. Is it a dolly, zoom, pan, track or hold? State subject pace independently; a slow camera can observe an urgent actor. Do not force an exact end image where none is needed. |
| Lighting and exposure | Actual sources/direction, softness, contrast/color and readable subjects. What comes from existing frames? What changes on entering another light zone or lifting a lit phone? A location sheet's neutral/daylight appearance may need a shot-specific night treatment. |
| Physical/temporal behaviour | Weight, contact, support, prop scale, rigid construction and occlusion. Normal speed or a source-supported exception; natural motion blur where relevant. Do not add slow motion, particles or jitter to make a shot seem cinematic. |
| Timing and edit boundary | Required action/reaction/lip timings from approved audio, usable interval, handles, entry/exit state and next-shot information. Keep editorial bookkeeping outside the model prompt when it provides no visible direction. |
| Workflow and technical output | Actual mode and supported inputs/settings, correct token binding, aspect ratio/resolution/duration/count, observed frame rate when supplied, actual quote/approval. Keep output specs separate from storytelling and acting. |

For an opening such as CO-01, the distinction is a gently moving camera with a hurried, eager actor. Body pace/lean/attention must carry that performance in a wide/rear view. A later close-up can carry the private smile; do not move that reaction earlier merely to add expression. Preserve established design while translating the tested staging into physical motion.

## Official guidance and its limits

- [Higgsfield camera control](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/ai-video-camera-control): distinguish camera movement/path/speed/easing from optical and lighting choices. Its full named parameter set is specific to Cinema Studio; verify the chosen route's actual controls.
- [Higgsfield Cinema Studio guide](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/cinema-studio-4-0-complete-guide): references, camera/lens/aperture, light and performance are separate decisions. Treat named presets as available controls or visual cues, not guarantees of perfect simulation. Do not switch the project's model/workflow without approval.
- [Midjourney Prompt Basics](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/32023408776205-Prompt-Basics): name important visible choices clearly; unspecified details are left to interpretation. [Midjourney Video](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/37460773864589-Video): global motion settings affect camera and character motion and can carry different risks. Those flags/settings are not transferable to Kling.
- [Runway's video prompting guide](https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/39789879462419-Gen-4-Video-Prompting-Guide): supplied images establish visual information; text can concentrate on subject, scene and camera motion with concrete positive descriptions. Keep its model-specific advice within its scope.

The user-supplied AI Video Generation Master Guide supports separating static structure from temporal performance. Its suggestions are not universal requirements: no mandatory neutral face, fixed emotional segment duration, blanket comma ban, model-independent motion slider, or rule that wide shots cannot express emotion. Recheck live provider documentation before relying on changing capabilities. Research informs decisions; current project approvals still control.
